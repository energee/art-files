You sit down with a master (the brand's template) and this beer's copy and art. Label Lab turns that into print-ready plates.

Export writes two PDFs with vector geometry and images at their original resolution:

- **Artwork** (`{name}.pdf`): the printed label, bleed included
- **Mask** (`{name}-mask.pdf`): the white-ink plate

On the mask, **blue is white ink** and **white is knockout**. Bare metallic stock shows through the holes. No mask image loaded? The plate floods full blue. That's "print this as a white label." Metallic type and marks still knock out to stock on top of the flood.

Need a designer in the loop? Export the same geometry as Photoshop files.

## The window

Three regions, like a real design tool:

1. **Layers** (left): master and version picker, every layer on this label
2. **Preview** (centre): the 2D sheet, the white-ink reveal, or a 3D can
3. **Inspector** (right): opens when you pick a layer. Content, type, position, colour

Tabs hold more than one project. Filling *this* beer never quietly rewrites the template. Moves, colours, and copy land on the label. The master only changes when you look at the review and hit save.
